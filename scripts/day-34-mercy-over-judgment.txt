
Peace be with you.
Welcome back to this Lenten journey —
a slow, honest walk toward the heart of God.
Wherever you are right now, take a breath…
let your spirit settle…
and let’s step into today together.


Today is Monday of the Fifth Week of Lent,
and Jesus speaks a word
that has the power to reshape a life:

“Neither do I condemn you.”

Not “I’ll think about it.”
Not “I’ll let it slide this time.”
Not “You should have known better.”

Just mercy.
Pure, unearned, undeserved mercy.

Today’s reflection says:

“Jesus meets you with mercy, not condemnation. His forgiveness restores dignity and opens the door to new beginnings. Lent teaches you to receive mercy and extend it to others.”

Mercy is the heartbeat of God.
It is the way Jesus looks at you —
not with disappointment,
not with frustration,
not with a list of your failures —
but with compassion.

Mercy restores what shame tries to destroy.
Mercy lifts your head
when guilt weighs it down.
Mercy opens the door
to a future you thought you lost.

Maybe there is something in your life
that still carries the echo of regret —
a choice you wish you could undo,
a moment you replay in your mind,
a wound you caused or received.

Jesus meets you there.
Not with condemnation,
but with mercy.

He does not define you by your past.
He does not measure you by your mistakes.
He does not hold you hostage
to the moments you wish you could change.

He restores your dignity.
He gives you back your name.
He opens the door to new beginnings.

And Lent invites you
not only to receive that mercy,
but to extend it —
to yourself,
and to others.

Mercy is not weakness.
It is strength.
The strength to let go.
The strength to forgive.
The strength to see others
as God sees them.

Let today be a day
where mercy becomes your posture —
toward yourself,
toward others,
toward the world.

So let us pray:

Lord, help me receive Your mercy
and offer it freely.
Heal what is wounded,
soften what is hardened,
and make my heart like Yours.
Amen.

Activity for today:  
Forgive someone silently in your heart today.

And sometime today —
maybe as you pause before speaking,
maybe while you’re walking from one room to another,
maybe in a moment that feels small and hidden —
just whisper:

“Lord, let mercy lead me.”

Let that whisper become your offering.

Thank you for walking this day of Lent with me.
May mercy shape your heart
and guide your steps
into new beginnings.
And until we meet again —
peace be with you.
